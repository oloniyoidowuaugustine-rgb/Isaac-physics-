Topics: Surface Tension & Viscous Force

1. Surface Tension

Surface tension is the property of a liquid that allows it to resist external force, due to the cohesive forces between its molecules.

It is responsible for phenomena such as:

  • Water forming droplets
  • Insects walking on water

Surface Tension (T) = Force / Length
Unit: N/m
Example: A wire loop of length 0.1 m experiences a force of 0.02 N at the liquid surface.
T = F / L = 0.02 / 0.1 = 0.2 N/m

2. Viscosity

Viscosity is the property of a fluid that resists its flow. High viscosity fluids (like honey) flow slowly, while low viscosity fluids (like water) flow quickly.

It depends on:

  • Temperature: higher temperature → lower viscosity
  • Nature of the liquid

Viscosity (η) = F × L / (A × v)
Where:
F = force, L = length, A = area, v = velocity
Example: A force of 5 N moves a plate over a liquid with area 2 m² at velocity 0.5 m/s and distance 0.2 m.
η = F × L / (A × v) = 5 × 0.2 / (2 × 0.5) = 1 N·s/m²
