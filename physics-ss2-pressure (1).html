Topic: Pressure in Fluids

1. Pressure

Pressure is the force applied per unit area.

Pressure (P) = Force (F) / Area (A)
Unit: Pascal (Pa)

2. Pressure in Liquids

Pressure at a point in a liquid at rest depends on:

  • Height of the liquid column (h)
  • Density of the liquid (ρ)
  • Acceleration due to gravity (g)

P = ρ × g × h

Where:
ρ = density of liquid (kg/m³)
g = acceleration due to gravity (≈ 9.8 m/s²)
h = height of liquid column (m)

3. Pressure in Gases

For a gas in a container, pressure is due to collisions of gas particles with the walls.

P = F / A

4. Pascal’s Principle

Pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted equally in all directions.

Example: A hydraulic lift has a small piston area of 0.01 m² and a large piston area of 1 m². Force applied on small piston = 100 N. Force on large piston = (Area ratio × Force) = (1 / 0.01) × 100 = 10000 N

5. Applications

  • Hydraulic lifts
  • Brakes in cars
  • Hydraulic presses
  • Dams and submarines
