Topic: Electromagnetic Induction

1. Definition

Electromagnetic induction is the production of an electromotive force (e.m.f) when there is a change in magnetic flux linking a conductor.

2. Magnetic Flux

Φ = B × A

Φ = magnetic flux (Weber)
B = magnetic field strength (Tesla)
A = area (m²)

3. Faraday’s Law

E = - N (ΔΦ / Δt)

E = induced e.m.f
N = number of turns
ΔΦ = change in flux
Δt = change in time

4. Lenz’s Law

The direction of induced e.m.f opposes the change producing it.

5. Applications

  • Electric generators
  • Transformers
  • Induction cookers
  • Bicycle dynamos

6. Example

Example: A coil of 200 turns experiences a flux change of 0.02 Wb in 0.5 s. E = - N (ΔΦ / Δt) E = -200 × (0.02 / 0.5) E = -200 × 0.04 = -8 V Induced e.m.f = 8 V (magnitude)

7. Notes

  • Induced current exists only when magnetic flux changes.
  • Generator works on electromagnetic induction principle.
  • Negative sign shows opposition (Lenz’s law).
