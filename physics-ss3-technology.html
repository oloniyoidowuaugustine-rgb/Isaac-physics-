Topic: Radioactivity & Nuclear Concepts

1. Radioactivity

Radioactivity is the spontaneous emission of radiation from unstable atomic nuclei.

2. Types of Radioactive Emission

  • Alpha (α) particles: He²⁺ nuclei, low penetration, stopped by paper.
  • Beta (β) particles: High-speed electrons, medium penetration, stopped by aluminum.
  • Gamma (γ) rays: Electromagnetic radiation, high penetration, stopped by thick lead.

3. Half-life

Half-life (T½) is the time taken for half of the radioactive nuclei to decay.

N = N₀ × (1/2)^(t/T½)

N = number of nuclei remaining
N₀ = initial number of nuclei
t = time elapsed

4. Nuclear Reactions

  • Fission: Splitting of a heavy nucleus into smaller nuclei, releasing energy (e.g., Uranium-235).
  • Fusion: Combining light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus, releasing huge energy (e.g., Sun’s energy).

5. Applications of Radioactivity

  • Medical imaging and cancer treatment
  • Dating of ancient objects (carbon dating)
  • Generation of nuclear power
  • Industrial radiography

6. Example

A sample has a half-life of 5 hours. If initial nuclei N₀ = 1000, after 10 hours: N = 1000 × (1/2)^(10/5) = 1000 × (1/2)² = 250 nuclei remaining

7. Notes

  • Radioactive decay is random but follows statistical laws.
  • Alpha particles are heavy, beta are light, gamma has no mass.
  • Fission and fusion are sources of nuclear energy.
