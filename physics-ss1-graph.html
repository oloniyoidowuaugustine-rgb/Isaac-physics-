Topic: Displacement & Velocity-Time Graph

1. Displacement

Displacement is the shortest distance between the initial and final position of an object in a given direction.

It is a vector quantity.

Displacement (s) = Final position - Initial position

2. Velocity

Velocity is the rate of change of displacement with time. It is a vector quantity.

Velocity (v) = Displacement (s) / Time (t)
Unit: m/s

3. Velocity-Time Graph

A velocity-time graph shows how the velocity of an object changes with time.

[Imagine a simple V-T graph here: velocity on Y-axis, time on X-axis]

Key Points:

  • Slope of V-T graph = Acceleration
  • Area under V-T graph = Displacement
  • Horizontal line → constant velocity
  • Line sloping upward → accelerating
  • Line sloping downward → decelerating

4. Worked Example

Example: A car moves at a constant velocity of 10 m/s for 5 seconds.
Displacement = v × t = 10 × 5 = 50 m
Graph: Horizontal line at 10 m/s from t = 0 to t = 5 s
Example: A car accelerates from 0 to 20 m/s in 10 seconds.
Acceleration = Δv / t = (20 - 0) / 10 = 2 m/s²
Area under graph = displacement = 0.5 × 20 × 10 = 100 m
