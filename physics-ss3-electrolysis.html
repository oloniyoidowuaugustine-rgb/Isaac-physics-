Topic: Electrolysis

1. Definition

Electrolysis is the process of breaking down a compound into its elements using an electric current passed through a molten or aqueous electrolyte.

2. Components of Electrolysis

  • Electrolyte: Substance that conducts electricity (ionic solution or molten salt).
  • Anode: Positive electrode where oxidation occurs.
  • Cathode: Negative electrode where reduction occurs.
  • Cations: Positive ions that move to the cathode.
  • Anions: Negative ions that move to the anode.

3. Faraday’s Laws of Electrolysis

First Law:

Mass of substance deposited ∝ Quantity of electricity passed.

m = Z × I × t

m = mass deposited (kg)
Z = electrochemical equivalent (kg/C)
I = current (A)
t = time (s)

Second Law:

When the same amount of electricity passes through different electrolytes, masses deposited are proportional to their chemical equivalents.

4. Example

A current of 3A passes through molten copper(II) sulfate for 10 minutes. If Z = 0.000329 kg/C, calculate the mass of copper deposited. Q = I × t = 3 × (10 × 60) = 1800 C
m = Z × Q = 0.000329 × 1800 ≈ 0.592 kg

5. Applications

  • Electroplating of metals (gold, silver, chromium)
  • Purification of metals (electro-refining)
  • Extraction of metals like aluminium
  • Production of gases like hydrogen and chlorine

6. Notes

  • Electrolysis occurs only in ionic compounds that are molten or in solution.
  • Cathode → reduction, Anode → oxidation
  • Mass deposited depends on current, time, and electrochemical equivalent.
