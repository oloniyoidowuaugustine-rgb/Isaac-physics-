Topic: Simple Machines

1. Definition

A simple machine is a device that **changes the magnitude or direction of a force** to make work easier.

2. Types of Simple Machines

  • Lever: A rigid bar pivoted on a fulcrum. Examples: seesaw, crowbar.
  • Pulley: A wheel with a rope to lift loads. Examples: flagpole, well bucket.
  • Inclined Plane: A sloping surface to raise objects. Examples: ramp, ladder.
  • Wheel and Axle: A wheel attached to a rod for lifting or moving. Examples: rolling pin, car steering wheel.
  • Screw: An inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder. Examples: jar lid, bolt.
  • Wedge: Two inclined planes joined to split objects. Examples: knife, axe.

3. Mechanical Advantage (MA)

Mechanical Advantage is the factor by which a machine **multiplies the applied force**.

MA = Load / Effort

Where:

  • Load = weight of the object being lifted
  • Effort = force applied to lift the load

4. Velocity Ratio (VR)

Velocity Ratio is the ratio of the **distance moved by the effort to the distance moved by the load**.

VR = Distance moved by effort / Distance moved by load

5. Efficiency

Efficiency tells how effective a machine is in converting **effort into useful work**.

Efficiency (%) = (MA / VR) × 100

6. Examples & Applications

  • Lever: Crowbar to lift heavy objects
  • Pulley: Hoisting water from a well
  • Inclined Plane: Loading goods into a truck
  • Wheel & Axle: Rolling barrels
  • Screw: Tightening jar lids or bolts
  • Wedge: Splitting wood with an axe
Example: A lever lifts a 200 N load with an effort of 50 N. MA = Load / Effort = 200 / 50 = 4
