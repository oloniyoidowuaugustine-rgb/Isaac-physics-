Topic: Refraction at Boundaries

1. Definition

Refraction is the **bending of light** as it passes from one transparent medium to another due to a change in speed.

2. Snell’s Law

n₁ sinθ₁ = n₂ sinθ₂

Where:
n₁ = refractive index of first medium
n₂ = refractive index of second medium
θ₁ = angle of incidence
θ₂ = angle of refraction

3. Refractive Index

Refractive index of a medium:

n = c / v
Where:
c = speed of light in vacuum
v = speed of light in medium

4. Phenomena due to Refraction

  • Apparent depth of objects in water
  • Bending of light in lenses
  • Prisms dispersing light into spectrum
  • Total internal reflection in optical fibers

5. Example

Example: Light passes from air (n₁=1) to water (n₂=1.33) at 30° incidence. Using Snell’s Law: sinθ₂ = n₁/n₂ × sinθ₁ = 1/1.33 × sin30° ≈ 0.375 → θ₂ ≈ 22°

6. Notes

  • Light bends towards the normal when entering a denser medium.
  • Light bends away from the normal when entering a rarer medium.
  • Critical angle occurs when refraction angle = 90°, leading to total internal reflection.
