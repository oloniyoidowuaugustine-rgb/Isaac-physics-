Topic: Position, Distance & Coordinate System

1. Position

The position of an object is its location relative to a reference point, usually called the origin.

2. Distance

Distance is the total length of the path travelled by an object. It is a **scalar quantity** (no direction).

3. Displacement

Displacement is the change in position of an object. It is a **vector quantity** (has magnitude and direction).

Displacement (s) = Final position - Initial position

4. Coordinate System

A coordinate system is a framework for specifying positions in space. Commonly used in physics:

  • 1D: x-axis (straight line)
  • 2D: x-y plane
  • 3D: x-y-z space

5. Examples

Example 1: A car moves from position 5 m to 15 m along a straight road. Distance travelled = 15 - 5 = 10 m Displacement = 15 - 5 = 10 m (since direction is along x-axis)
Example 2: A person walks 5 m east, then 3 m west. Distance = 5 + 3 = 8 m Displacement = 5 - 3 = 2 m east

6. Notes

  • Distance is always positive; displacement can be positive or negative depending on direction.
  • Coordinate systems help in describing motion in multiple dimensions.
