Topic: Expansion of Gases & Gas Laws

1. Expansion of Gases

Gases expand to fill the volume of the container because their particles move freely in all directions. Expansion occurs when gases are heated (temperature increases) or pressure decreases.

2. Charles’ Law (Volume-Temperature Relationship)

At constant pressure, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature.

V ∝ T → V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂

3. Boyle’s Law (Pressure-Volume Relationship)

At constant temperature, the pressure of a fixed mass of gas is inversely proportional to its volume.

P ∝ 1/V → P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

4. Gay-Lussac’s Law (Pressure-Temperature Relationship)

At constant volume, the pressure of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature.

P ∝ T → P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂

5. Combined Gas Law

Combines Boyle’s, Charles’, and Gay-Lussac’s laws:

(P₁V₁)/T₁ = (P₂V₂)/T₂

6. Examples

Example 1: A gas occupies 2 L at 300 K. Temperature rises to 450 K at constant pressure. V₂ = V₁ × T₂/T₁ = 2 × 450/300 = 3 L
Example 2: A gas in a cylinder has P₁ = 100 kPa, V₁ = 4 L. Pressure changes to 200 kPa at constant temperature. V₂ = P₁V₁ / P₂ = (100 × 4)/200 = 2 L
