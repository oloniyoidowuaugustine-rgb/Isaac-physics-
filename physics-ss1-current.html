Topic: Electric Current

1. Meaning of Electric Current

Electric current is the flow of electric charges (usually electrons) through a conductor.

In metals, electric current is caused by the movement of free electrons.

Electric Current (I) = Charge (Q) / Time (t)

I = Q / t

Unit of current = Ampere (A)

1 Ampere is the current when 1 Coulomb of charge flows in 1 second.

1 A = 1 C / 1 s

2. Types of Electric Current

(a) Direct Current (DC)

Direct current flows in one direction only. Example: Battery.

(b) Alternating Current (AC)

Alternating current changes direction continuously. Example: Electricity supplied to homes.

3. Electric Circuit

An electric circuit is a complete closed path through which current flows.

For current to flow, the circuit must be closed.

Basic Circuit Components:

  • Cell or Battery (source of power)
  • Switch
  • Connecting wires
  • Load (bulb, resistor, fan)

4. Measuring Electric Current

Electric current is measured using an instrument called an Ammeter.

An ammeter is connected in series in a circuit.

5. Potential Difference (Voltage)

Potential difference is the work done in moving a unit charge from one point to another in a circuit.

V = W / Q

Unit of voltage = Volt (V)

Voltage is measured using a Voltmeter. A voltmeter is connected in parallel.

6. Resistance

Resistance is the opposition to the flow of electric current.

Unit of resistance = Ohm (Ω)

7. Ohm's Law

Ohm’s Law states that the current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it, provided temperature remains constant.

V = IR

Where:
V = Voltage
I = Current
R = Resistance

Example: If V = 12V and R = 4Ω,
I = V / R
I = 12 / 4
I = 3A

8. Factors Affecting Resistance

  • Length of conductor
  • Cross-sectional area
  • Temperature
  • Type of material

9. Electric Power

Electric power is the rate at which electrical energy is used.

Power (P) = VI

Unit of power = Watt (W)

Example: If V = 10V and I = 2A
P = 10 × 2 = 20W
