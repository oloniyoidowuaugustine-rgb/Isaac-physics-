Topic: Force, Momentum & Newton's Laws of Motion

1. Force

A force is a push or pull on an object that can cause it to change its state of motion or shape.

Unit of force: Newton (N)

1 N = 1 kg × 1 m/s²

2. Momentum

Momentum is the product of mass and velocity of an object. It is a vector quantity.

Momentum (p) = Mass (m) × Velocity (v)
Unit: kg·m/s
Example: A car of mass 1000 kg moving at 20 m/s. Momentum = 1000 × 20 = 20000 kg·m/s

3. Newton's Laws of Motion

3.1 First Law (Law of Inertia)

An object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a resultant force.

3.2 Second Law

The rate of change of momentum of an object is directly proportional to the force applied and takes place in the direction of the force.

F = m × a

3.3 Third Law

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Example: When you push a wall, the wall pushes back with the same force in the opposite direction.

4. Applications of Newton's Laws

  • Seat belts in cars (First law)
  • Rocket launching (Third law)
  • Pushing objects (Second law)
