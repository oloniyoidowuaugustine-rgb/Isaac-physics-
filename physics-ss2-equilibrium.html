Topic: Force and Equilibrium of Particles

1. Definition

Equilibrium of a particle occurs when a particle is **at rest or moving with constant velocity**, and the **net force acting on it is zero**.

2. Conditions for Equilibrium

  • Vector sum of all forces = 0: ΣF = 0
  • The particle remains stationary or continues moving uniformly.

3. Types of Equilibrium

  • Stable Equilibrium: Particle returns to original position when displaced.
  • Unstable Equilibrium: Particle moves further away when displaced.
  • Neutral Equilibrium: Particle remains in new position after displacement.

4. Resolution of Forces

Forces acting at a point can be resolved into perpendicular components to simplify equilibrium analysis:

ΣFx = 0, ΣFy = 0

5. Example

Example: A particle is acted upon by two forces 10 N east and 10 N west. ΣF = 10 - 10 = 0 → Particle is in equilibrium
Example 2: A 5 N force acts at 0° and 12 N at 90°. Resultant: R = √(5² + 12²) = √169 = 13 N Direction: θ = tan⁻¹(12/5) ≈ 67.38° For equilibrium, a third force of 13 N in opposite direction is needed

6. Notes

  • Equilibrium analysis is essential in statics problems.
  • Use vector resolution to solve problems with forces at angles.
  • Both magnitude and direction must be considered.
