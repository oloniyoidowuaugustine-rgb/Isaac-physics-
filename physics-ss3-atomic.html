Topic: Atomic Models & Atomic Spectra

1. Dalton’s Model

  • Atoms are indivisible and indestructible.
  • Atoms of the same element are identical.
  • Atoms combine in simple ratios to form compounds.

2. Thomson’s Model

  • Also called “Plum Pudding Model”.
  • Atom is a positive sphere with electrons embedded in it.

3. Rutherford’s Model

  • Atom has a tiny, dense positively charged nucleus.
  • Electrons move around nucleus in empty space.

4. Bohr’s Model

  • Electrons move in fixed circular orbits around nucleus.
  • Energy levels are quantized.
  • Electrons can jump between levels by absorbing or emitting energy.

5. Atomic Spectra

When atoms absorb or emit energy, they produce specific lines called atomic spectra.

  • Line Spectrum: Discrete colored lines on dark background.
  • Continuous Spectrum: All colors without gaps.

6. Examples

Example: Hydrogen emits light at 656 nm (red), 486 nm (blue-green), etc. These are called Balmer series lines.

7. Notes

  • Atomic spectra are used to identify elements.
  • Bohr’s model explains the line spectra of hydrogen.
  • Rutherford’s model could not explain spectral lines.
