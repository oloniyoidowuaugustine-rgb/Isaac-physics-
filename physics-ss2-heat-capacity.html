Topic: Heat Capacity & Specific Heat

1. Heat Capacity (C)

Heat capacity is the amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of an object by 1°C (or 1 K).

C = Q / ΔT

Where:
Q = Heat absorbed (Joules)
ΔT = Change in temperature (°C or K)
Unit: Joules per °C (J/°C)

2. Specific Heat Capacity (c)

Specific heat capacity is the amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of **1 kg of a substance by 1°C**.

c = Q / (m × ΔT)

Where:
m = mass of the substance (kg)
Q = Heat absorbed (J)
ΔT = Temperature change (°C or K)
Unit: J/kg°C

3. Examples

Example 1: A 2 kg iron block is heated by 600 J. Temperature rises by 3°C. c = Q / (m × ΔT) = 600 / (2 × 3) = 100 J/kg°C
Example 2: A substance absorbs 5000 J to raise its 5 kg mass by 10°C. c = 5000 / (5 × 10) = 100 J/kg°C

4. Notes

  • Substances with high specific heat (e.g., water) heat up slowly.
  • Substances with low specific heat (e.g., metals) heat up quickly.
  • Specific heat is useful in calculating energy needed for heating/cooling objects.
