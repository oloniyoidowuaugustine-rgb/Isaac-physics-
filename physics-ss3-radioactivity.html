SS3 Physics – Radioactivity

Isaac Physics | Complete Lesson Note

1. Meaning of Radioactivity

Radioactivity is the spontaneous disintegration of unstable atomic nuclei accompanied by the emission of radiation.

The process is natural and cannot be controlled by chemical or physical means.

2. Types of Radioactive Radiation

Alpha (α) Particles

  • Consist of 2 protons and 2 neutrons
  • Positively charged (+2)
  • Low penetrating power
  • Stopped by paper

Beta (β) Particles

  • Fast-moving electrons
  • Negatively charged (-1)
  • Medium penetrating power
  • Stopped by thin aluminium sheet

Gamma (γ) Rays

  • Electromagnetic waves
  • No mass and no charge
  • Very high penetrating power
  • Stopped by thick lead or concrete

3. Comparison of Alpha, Beta and Gamma

Radiation Charge Mass Penetration Deflection
Alpha +2 Heavy Low Slight
Beta -1 Very small Medium Strong
Gamma 0 None Very High None

4. Radioactive Decay

Radioactive decay occurs when an unstable nucleus emits radiation to become more stable.

Example of Alpha Decay:

U → Th + α

²³⁸U → ²³⁴Th + ⁴₂He

5. Half-Life

Half-life is the time taken for half of a radioactive substance to decay.

Formula:

Remaining Quantity = Initial × (1/2)n

Example:

  • Initial mass = 100g
  • After 1 half-life = 50g
  • After 2 half-lives = 25g
  • After 3 half-lives = 12.5g

6. Uses of Radioactivity

  • Medical treatment (Cancer therapy)
  • Carbon dating
  • Electricity generation
  • Industrial thickness measurement

7. Dangers of Radioactivity

  • Cell damage
  • Cancer
  • Genetic mutation
  • Radiation sickness
