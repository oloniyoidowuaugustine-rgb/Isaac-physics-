Linear Expansivity

Linear expansion occurs in solid because of the rigidity and the intermolecular forces of attraction of the solid.When a solid is heated to a particular temperature, the molecules it contain absorb some kinetic energy, since the kinetic energy is the measure of velocity

Linear expansivity of solid is defined as the increase in length per unit rise in temperature. It is denoted by a and measured in K-¹ or C-¹(SI unit)

a=L2-L1/L1∆ø

Example:If a metal rod of length 50m expands when heated at a temperature change of 10k, find the change in length (linear expansivity of metal=1.5×10-⁸k-¹)

    a=L2-L1/L1∆ø
    ∆L=L2-L1∆ø
    ∆L=L2-L1=1.5×10-⁸×10×50
    ∆Ø=L2-L1=750×10-⁸
    ∆ø=L2-L1=7.5×10-⁶m
    .: Change in length is 7.5×10-⁶m
