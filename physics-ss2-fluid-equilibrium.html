Topic: Equilibrium in Fluids

1. Definition

A fluid (liquid or gas) is in equilibrium when it is **at rest** or **moving uniformly** and the **pressure at any point is the same in all directions**.

2. Pressure in Fluids

Pressure at a depth h in a fluid of density ρ under gravity g:

P = P₀ + ρ g h

Where:
P = pressure at depth h
P₀ = atmospheric pressure at surface
ρ = density of fluid
g = acceleration due to gravity
h = depth

3. Principle of Equilibrium

  • Pressure is transmitted equally in all directions (Pascal’s principle)
  • A body submerged in a fluid experiences an upthrust equal to the weight of displaced fluid (Archimedes’ principle)
  • Fluids at rest exert perpendicular forces on surfaces

4. Example

Example 1: A liquid of density 1000 kg/m³ is 2 m deep. Pressure at bottom: P = ρ g h = 1000 × 9.8 × 2 = 19600 Pa
Example 2: A cube of volume 0.01 m³ is submerged in water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³). Upthrust = ρ g V = 1000 × 9.8 × 0.01 = 98 N

5. Notes

  • Fluids cannot resist shear, only normal (perpendicular) forces.
  • Pressure increases with depth.
  • Equilibrium ensures no net motion or deformation of fluid.
