Topic: Uniformly Accelerated Motion (UAM)

1. Definition

Uniformly Accelerated Motion (UAM) occurs when an object moves in a straight line with **constant acceleration**.

2. Key Formulas

  • v = u + at
  • s = ut + ½ at²
  • v² = u² + 2as

Where:
u = initial velocity (m/s)
v = final velocity (m/s)
a = acceleration (m/s²)
t = time (s)
s = displacement (m)

3. Graphs

  • Velocity-Time graph: slope = acceleration, area = displacement
  • Displacement-Time graph: parabolic shape for UAM

4. Examples

Example 1: A car starts from rest (u = 0) and accelerates at 2 m/s² for 5 s. Final velocity: v = u + at = 0 + (2 × 5) = 10 m/s Displacement: s = ut + ½ at² = 0 + ½ × 2 × 5² = 25 m
Example 2: A vehicle accelerates from 10 m/s to 30 m/s over a distance of 100 m. Acceleration: v² = u² + 2as → 30² = 10² + 2 × a × 100 → 900 = 100 + 200a → a = 4 m/s²

5. Notes

  • Acceleration is constant in UAM.
  • Direction matters: positive for increasing velocity, negative for deceleration.
  • Formulas can be applied in any straight-line motion problem.
